On the hobby side, I have spent most of my week working on my DnD setting and a campaign module I am working on. For those in the know, it's based on an earlier campaign I ran, the Fenusia campaign. I am still quite fond of that campaign, but it was flawed, and it eventually ended unfinished.

Where the original campaign started as a sea exploration campaign which eventually became a conspiracy campaign, I am reimagining it as a campaign which balances the sea-exploration and the conspiracy aspects by essentially being a hexcrawl campaign which switches between raising money for expeditions (interacting with the city and its conspiracies) and actually crawling the hexes on a boat (sea-exploration). I also added a new focus on curses, to add in more spooky elements. The campaign will be from level 1 to level 11.

Currently, I have a good setup for the player paths of the campaign and I am redesigning the city of Fenusia (partly based on Venice), including the events that happen in the city during the campaign. These events and the design of the city will then be used to create questlines for the party to make money off of for their expeditions, through which the party can also affect the city.

During initial stages of design, I used a bit of ChatGPT to rubber duck some of my ideas, as almost everyone I play DnD with is also somehow involved in the setting and I did not want to give them spoilers, but I also like to discuss ideas in some manner. ChatGPT is however somewhat limited in this respect (for one, its a yes-man, which I do not need) and it doesn't replace discussing this stuff with actual people, I caved this week and asked two friends to do some proofreading and to help me along a little.

I am also still learning Frama-C at work. For anyone learning Frama-C, I recommend [the tutorial by Allan Blanchard](https://allan-blanchard.fr/publis/frama-c-wp-tutorial-en.pdf), which I am quite enjoying so far. I did run into some trouble with getting Frama-C to list CVC5 as a Prover, but maybe this will be solved tomorrow.
In other work news, I got some more tasks, which I find quite exciting.
