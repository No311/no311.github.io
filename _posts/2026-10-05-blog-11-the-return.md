Last Sunday, I returned from Bangkok, after visiting Bram, my good friend. 
As we were coming in to land, I looked out of the window, and I realized I had arrived in Singapore.
As I had also booked the flight from Bangkok to Singapore, this was not a large surprise, but somehow, the return felt fresh.
For the first time in a while, Singapore felt full of possibility.

The week before the return was good. Excellent, even. However, and as you may have noticed from the shorter blog posts, I had been in a funk for a few weeks already.
The funk was partly just a bit of home-sickness, but it was partly work-related as well.
The details are not suited to a blog, but Monday the 28th some circumstances changed that largely resolved the direct causes for the funk.
I would like to say that I immediately felt better, but in truth the rest of the week was spent somewhat overthinking things, as I am prone to do.

As a reaction, and as a result of the resolution, I made sure to have fun during the week by planning fun stuff, to prevent me from shutting myself in too much. I also made sure to have several discussions with family and some friends over the past few weeks.
This allowed me to fight back against the feeling that Doom was upon me, and have some fun while doing so.

On Thursday 1st, I took things a bit more easy and recharged a bit.

On Friday 2nd, I worked from home. Generally, working from home is better for creativity and focus when I have a goal, while working on location is better for setting goals and finding resources. I still have to train it a bit, but I think it is good to put this desk to use and work from home once a week.

The evening of Friday the 2nd, I went to Bangkok. I was at the airport far too early, but then, I had also forgotten to pack some underwear, so I had reason to explore the overly expensive airport shops. At 20:50, the plane went to Bangkok, and when it arrived, I booked a taxi to Bram's place.
Though I was ready to sleep, Bram convinced me to watch White Chicks before we turned in; what followed was a fairly relaxing and entertaining late evening. Though the movie is not something I would normally watch, it was fantastic. If you like zany plots and an excellent comedic turn by Terry Crews, White Chicks is one to check out.

On Saturday, we pulled out all the stops with regards to relaxation; Bram had made some plans, and they were excellent. After some time, I finally fully began to relax.
The highlight was a visit to the Tribe, an excellent bar in Bangkok; maybe even the best bar I have had the pleasure to visit.
There, we enjoyed fruit, a nice south african sparkling wine (it turns out that reservations in Bangkok require a certain amount of spending, which is effective policy) and we met some cool people from Thailand and South America.

We also went to a whisky and cigar lounge, which was quite excellent, and we reminisced a bit (as one does when sipping rum and smoking a cigar). 
Thanks to my father's and Bram's tips, smoking the cigar went quite well; I may do it again in the future, on special occasions.

On Sunday, we took it easy. Me and Bram discussed life a bit (a very good conversation) and we went on a small excursion to find brunch. After some more chilling, I then returned to the airport for my flight

The weekend was truly excellent, and I would like to take this opportunity to thank Bram again for it. The Saturday especially features high on my list of favourite days in the past few years. I had forgotten one could live that much in just a day, and I am happy to have been reminded.

It is funny to realize, now that my overthinking is proportional to the events of the week again, that I didn't have to change much about my actions to have an effective week.
I had been putting too much focus on results to measure effectiveness, while I am not someone that can force results out of myself by putting myself under pressure beyond a certain limit.
This week, however, the effort I had put into broadening my horizons to fit the current main project seems to be starting to pay off, showing me that not every effect needs to be immediate once again.

In the evening, I met with Ferry and Bob for AuDaLa; Ferry is doing good work there, and we planned a day to meet when I am back in the Netherlands.
I also considered how to solve my one mistake in the thesis. 

I came to the conclusion that to solve it, I would need to add a set *A* of AuDaLogic variables to AuDaLogic triples. These variables are then used to define an equivalence class relative to the AuDaLogic variable environment:
for every environment *s*, any environment in the equivalence class over *s* and *A* can differ from *s* only in the values of the variables *A*.
An AuDaLogic triple with set *A* is then only valid for a state with AuDaLogic environment *s* if it is valid for any state that replaces *s* with an AuDaLogic environment from the equivalence class over *s* and *A*.
This would allow us to abstract away from exact values for *i* in the Gen Parallel proof rule and fix the rule.

Doing something like this is not a new idea, as similar things have been done in logics in the past.
In the end, I decided to not implement it, as it requires a substantial rewrite of chapters 7, 8 and 9 of my thesis, which I do not think is something to prioritize right now. I may do so later, and depending on what happens with AuDaLa, I will include this in any second version of the logic.
