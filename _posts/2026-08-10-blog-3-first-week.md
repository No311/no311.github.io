Well, that's my first week of work done. And wouldn't you know it: immediate long weekend! Turns out that Singapore turns 61; clearly, this means that in about 4 to 8 years, Singapore is eligible for retirement.
Somehow, that seems unlikely to happen, but I will be following Singapore's life choices with great interest.
![Singapopcorn]({{ site.baseurl }}/content/Blog3/Singapopcorn.jpg){: style="display:block; margin:auto; width:200px; height:auto; border-radius:50px; padding: 20px;"}

Stupid jokes aside, I was pretty happy with that extra day of rest; I am still acclimatizing, and this gave me an extra day in which I could focus on living in Singapore and not also at the same time getting myself up to speed with work.

Turns out it was needed, I spent most of the day reading in bed and playing and learning chess with and from Patrick. I am not particularly good at it, but I think I can see the shape of good chess play now, and perhaps I will get better soon.
I am considering to get the Chess app for a year too; might be a fun skill to learn.

So, on work: I have, by now, truly met four people of the group and spoken to another few members.
The social element of being part of a research group is far less pronounced than it was at the TU/e, which is something to get used to.
However, the group seems to be on top of things with regards to research, and I am confident that I can learn a lot here; now it is up to me to find something I can add to the group's efforts.

Furthermore, I estimate that I will be able to enjoy working with at least those I have met, and our lunches together so far have been fun and relaxing.
Thank you Eduardo for letting Martin know I was going to Singapore.
I have an ambition to see how many of the group I can get to join me for lunch, but I have been reliably informed that getting everyone to join a lunch at some point is unlikely to succeed.
This will, of course, not stop me for now.

This week, I spent familiarizing myself with the details of C using the book Modern C (thanks Ferry!) and the basics of Frama-C, using a book and the website.
![bread]({{ site.baseurl }}/content/Blog3/modernc.jpg){: style="float: right; padding: 10px 10px 10px 10px; width: 300px;border-radius:50px;"}
C so far is frustrating to me, due to its continued inclusion of historical design choices due to the fact that C is an older language (like the difference between strings and char arrays).
The book also has some conventions about programming in C for which the rationale is basically "Just accept this, historically it was done like this."
As someone who has, since his PhD, been focusing on recultivating his curiosity and considers that to be an absolutely bogus reason for the existence of conventions, this does not endear the language to me.
Maybe at some point I shall make a little post about my experiences in C programming and analyzing.

On the bureaucratic side, the end of the tunnel is visible.
I now have a Singpass, and my Employee Pass (visa) will arrive at the university next week.
My address was not correctly communicated to the Ministry of Manpower; this was not debilitating but it sent me on a little sidequest to get that fixed, which ended today when the landlord gave permission for an Employee Pass to be registered at my current address.
Furthermore, my bank account works and I have been able to pay all my renting obligations.
This did not stop the company from which I rent to send me an invoice the day after I paid the obligations.
I tried to contact them through four to six different channels, from which I learnt the following lesson:

**Singapore Lesson 2:** No news is good news. Do not expect to get confirmation mails for contacting services.

I also had some problems with 2FA authentication; this I had to fix by reinstalling Windows Authenticator, which conveniently defenestrated my 2FA authentication for the TU/e, to which I think I will still have access until February, and to which I more importantly would like to keep access until February as I am the corresponding author for at least some of the AuDaLa papers.
I have contacted the TU/e, but they can only reset my 2FA through a meeting, which I will pick back up when my phone issues are done.

Phone-wise, with my Singpass, I can officially register my phone number. Of course, this could not be done through the app, because it turns out Singpass has some trouble communicating one's nationality to other services when one is Dutch, so today, I visited two different locations of the Singtel phone company to get my phone registered to me.

This should be processed on Wednesday, after which I can transfer my phone to eSim, after which I should try to call the employee of the phone company to check whether I can place outgoing calls, which I for some arcane reason am not able to do (this seems to not be the standard). Then, I should call the phone company for 5G access (I should already have it, but I don't).

It has been an interesting week.
I have noticed that I am still conceptualizing this adventure as a short-term temporary thing, but I am slowly realizing that this is going to be for at least a year, and a year is a long time.
This has for now led me to the conclusion that I do not want to miss two years of Poekie's life, so if I stay for longer Poekie will have to come here.
Secondly, I acclimatize reasonably well to moving abroad. I mean sure, I have given an exposition on all the trouble it has been so far, but I find I don't truly feel like the trouble was a massive imposition; at the moment, it is an amusing annoyance.
